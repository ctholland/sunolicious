The Full Spectrum

Not just professionals.
Everyone who creates.

Technology has made everyone a creator — and that's the point. The open door is what makes Suno remarkable. But within that open door, there are levels. Both deserve a home.

The Casual Creator

Making music for the joy of it.

They're not thinking about publishing rights or sync deals. They made something they love and want to share it. Suno Connect gives them an audience. Suno Radio gives them airplay. Connect Live puts them in a room with people who get it. This is the culture — and culture is the foundation everything else is built on.

The Power User

Building a catalog. Ready when the moment comes.

They know what a publishing split is. They register their compositions. They're using Suno as a serious creative and production tool — and they need the platform to give them visibility, opportunity, and a direct path to licensing revenue. Suno Sync is that path. The legal infrastructure sits where it always has — with the songwriter's PRO, their registrations, their agreements. Suno provides a music collaboration partner and a marketplace. The creator brings the work.

02 — Suno Sync

The Marketplace.

Film. TV. Ads. Games. Brands. The world needs music every day. Suno Sync puts creators in the room where those deals actually happen.

02.1 — Opportunity Board

Live sync briefs, updated daily.

Music supervisors post briefs — genre, mood, budget, deadline. Creators browse and pitch relevant tracks directly. No middleman. No guesswork.

02.2 — AI-Assisted Matching

The right track finds the right brief.

Suno's audio model already understands music deeply. Use that intelligence to surface creator tracks matching a supervisor's brief — automatically, before they even search.

02.3 — Songwriter Verification

PRO affiliation. ISRC. Real infrastructure.

Legitimate sync licensing runs on two rails — PRO affiliation (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC) for performance royalties, and ISRC codes for tracking plays. Suno Sync builds around these existing standards. Songwriters provide what the industry already recognizes. The platform honors it.

02.4 — Licensing Framework

Deal in hours, not months.

Standard licensing agreements built in — non-exclusive sync and term licenses are the clear starting point. Buyouts require more careful consideration in this context: when a track's production layer was generated by an AI model trained on existing works, the full scope of what's being sold needs to be clearly defined. That framework is still being worked out industry-wide. Suno Sync builds around what's clear now, and evolves as the standards settle.

04 — Suno Radio

The Signal.

Suno's discover feed already points toward something bigger. The difference between autoplay and a station is curation, creator attribution, and a potential revenue model. That's what Radio builds toward.

04.1 — Always-On Listening

A lean-back experience that's missing.

Suno is already moving toward a lean-back experience — CarPlay, curated playlists, passive discovery. Radio is what that becomes when it's fully programmed: creator-attributed streams by mood, genre, time of day. The casual creator gets airplay. The power user gets discovered. Everyone stays longer.

04.2 — Owned Monetization

Not Spotify's table. Suno's table.

The DSP model has never worked for independent artists. Pennies per stream, algorithms that favor established acts, zero relationship with the platform. Suno Radio is a chance to build something different — direct creator payouts, set by Suno, on a platform Suno owns. The infrastructure to get there isn't trivial. But the direction is right.

04.3 — Discovery Engine

Radio feeds everything else.

A listener hears something on Suno Radio, follows the creator on Suno Connect, sees them perform on Connect Live, and a music supervisor discovers them through Suno Sync. Radio is the top of the funnel for the entire ecosystem — passive, always-on, always working.

04.4 — Something to Build Toward

Not a promise. A direction.

Licensing at scale, PRO relationships, operational infrastructure — this takes time and manpower to do properly. But Suno Radio doesn't have to launch fully formed. It starts as a discovery feature and grows into a revenue layer as the platform matures. The vision is worth naming now.

The DSP Problem

Spotify pays between $0.003 and $0.005 per stream. For an independent artist to earn $1,000 a month, they need roughly 250,000 streams. That's the system. Suno Radio doesn't have to replicate it.

PRO + ISRC — The Real Infrastructure

Legitimate music monetization runs on two rails: PRO affiliation (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC) for performance royalties, and ISRC codes for tracking plays across platforms. These are the practical building blocks of any creator payment system — not copyright registration, which is litigation insurance, not a licensing requirement. Suno Radio builds on this foundation.

The Labels Are Already Getting Their Cut

The Warner deal signals where this is going. Once revenue sharing is established, the litigation incentive largely disappears. The industry moves from fighting AI to taxing it. Suno Radio — done properly — is how Suno builds something the labels can participate in and creators actually get paid from.

The platform that names what everyone already hears is the platform that earns the trust of the people who made it possible.

The Manifesto

Humans made the music.
That never changed.

AI didn't create music. It modeled what humans spent centuries building — every melody, every lyric, every chord progression, every rhythmic pattern. The composition is human. It always was. The law is catching up to what songwriters have always known.

US Copyright Office · Jan 2025

Human authorship = copyright.

"AI-generated work can be copyrighted when it embodies meaningful human authorship." Purely algorithmic output falls into the public domain.

US Court of Appeals · Mar 2025

AI alone can't own a copyright.

Landmark ruling denied copyright for works generated without meaningful human involvement. The creative human is the author — not the tool.

PRS / Industry Standard

Melody + lyrics = your IP.

Where a songwriter writes melody and lyrics and uses AI for production, the work qualifies for copyright registration. The composition is the asset.

The Gift

Access to collective musical knowledge.

Every musical tradition, every genre, every production technique developed over centuries of human creativity — AI has modeled all of it. A songwriter in a bedroom in the Bronx now has access to the same harmonic vocabulary as someone who studied at Berklee for four years. A musician anywhere can draw on production knowledge that used to require years of training and expensive studio time. Even with a subscription cost. Even with a potential label tax on the horizon. The access is worth it.

Suno's PR Opportunity

Every AI company is fighting the doomsday narrative. Suno doesn't have to.

Water supply fears. Job displacement. Loss of human agency. AI is carrying that weight culturally right now. But music is the most human thing there is — and Suno's entire platform exists to help humans make more of it. That's not the AI doomsday story. That's the opposite. The Human Machine Manifest is that counter-narrative, built in. Suno positions itself as human-centric not just as a legal defense — but as a genuine cultural contribution.

A Creator's Honest Position on Splits

When a songwriter feeds a complete production into Suno — original composition, lyrics, melody, existing copyright — and what comes back is genuinely better, a fair exchange is worth having. That's not a 50/50 split. The songwriter's original creative work is the foundation everything is built on. The AI contribution is the processing and the collective knowledge layer. The split should reflect that.

Nobody knows exactly what those numbers look like yet — that's being worked out in courtrooms and boardrooms right now. But the principle is clear: the human creative input is the most valuable part. The platform that honors that — and builds its economics around it — is the one songwriters will trust.

The Moral Argument

AI didn't create the inequality.
It inherited it.

Session musicians were already being replaced by samples. Live bands by loops. Studios by bedroom producers. The industry was doing that long before AI arrived. A songwriter contributing lyrics and melody, a vocalist who phrased a line in a way nobody had before, a musician who developed a chord voicing that became a reference — AI modeled all of them. Every contributor in that chain. Most of whom were never going to get hired anyway because the budget wasn't there.

So the question isn't whether AI replaces the musician. The question is whether the platform built on top of all that human creative contribution finally gives those people something back — access, recognition, a cut. A songwriter who couldn't afford a string section now has one. A vocalist who couldn't afford a producer now has one. The collective knowledge becomes the equalizer.

That's not AI taking jobs.
That's AI giving access to people who were already locked out.
Build the platform that honors that distinction.

Why This Matters for Suno

Suno settled with Warner Music in November 2025 — a landmark partnership that includes licensed models and Suno's acquisition of Songkick. Universal and Sony are still in active litigation, with a pivotal fair-use ruling expected in summer 2026. The legal picture is moving fast. The platform that gets ahead of it — building a genuinely creator-first model before the dust settles — is the one that emerges with the trust of the music industry and the artists inside it. Suno Connect, Suno Sync, and Suno Amplify are that platform.
